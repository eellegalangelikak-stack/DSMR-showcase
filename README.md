## The Wonder in Small Things

Every day, we move through countless moments of effort, intention, and accomplishment.

Some are extraordinary. Others are almost invisible.

We finish a task. We make a decision. We take one small step forward.

Then, often without noticing, we move on.

But what if completion could become an experience worth remembering?

**DSMR™ — Dopamine Satisfaction Micro-Ritual** explores that question.

The concept investigates how thoughtful interaction, sensory feedback, and small moments of acknowledgment might influence the way people experience progress.

It begins with a belief worth exploring:

*Meaningful progress doesn’t always have to be monumental. Sometimes, it simply needs to be noticed.*

---

## Where Creativity Meets Inquiry

DSMR™ is being developed through two connected directions.

### ✨ Interactive Experience

Exploring how visual expression, tactile interaction, and intentional moments of celebration might change the experience of completing everyday actions.

The creative ambition is to make interaction feel expressive, intuitive, and personally meaningful.

### 🧠 Human-Centered Research

Exploring questions at the intersection of interaction design, motivation, sensory feedback, and the subjective experience of accomplishment.

This direction emphasizes careful observation, thoughtful design, and responsible testing.

**The aim is to investigate possibilities—not assume outcomes.**

---

## Guiding Principles

**01 — Small moments matter.**

Progress deserves acknowledgment at every scale.

**02 — Experience is part of design.**

How an interaction feels can be as interesting to investigate as what it accomplishes.

**03 — Curiosity precedes certainty.**

Ideas invite questions. Research and testing help determine what those ideas can genuinely support.

**04 — Utility and wonder can coexist.**

Practical technology can also offer delight, imagination, and thoughtful expression.

**05 — Human experience comes first.**

The purpose of exploration is to better understand how people encounter, interpret, and experience technology.
